Research

My research sits at the intersection of computer vision and autonomous navigation -- making UGVs understand unstructured terrain well enough to drive through it safely.

Primary Focus

Semantic Terrain Segmentation for Off-Road Navigation

Off-road environments don't have lane markings or curbs -- the robot has to figure out what's drivable (packed dirt, gravel) vs what's not (tall grass, mud, water) purely from visual data. That's the problem I'm working on.

I started with GANav (built on MiT-B0), but discovered that its core group-wise attention mechanism was being bypassed during training. Instead of patching it, I migrated to a clean SegFormer MiT-B2 backbone within the MMSegmentation framework. Full fine-tuning on approximately 6,500 labeled images reached around 52 mIoU, with cross-dataset generalization tested on RUGD and RELLIS-3D.

This involved building a custom AVMIDataset class with RGB mask-to-index conversion, deep-diving into SegFormer's hierarchical transformer architecture (patch embeddings, efficient self-attention), and understanding the tradeoffs in preprocessing and input resizing for dense prediction tasks.

Sensor Fusion

LiDAR-Camera Fusion Pipeline

Cameras give you rich color and texture, but no depth. LiDAR gives you precise 3D geometry, but no color. Fusing them means the robot gets both -- it can see what type of terrain is at each 3D point in space.

I built and debugged a ROS2-based fusion pipeline that projects LiDAR point clouds onto camera images. The tricky part was handling the coordinate frame mismatch: the simulation environment (Unreal Engine) uses a left-handed coordinate system, while ROS2 uses right-handed. I also implemented timestamp synchronization between 30Hz camera frames and 10Hz LiDAR scans, built an interactive calibration tuner, and wrote tooling to extract and organize data from ROS2 bag files.
